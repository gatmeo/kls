\section{Introduction}

Kazhdan-Lustig-Stanley (KLS) polynomials and Chow polynomials are elements in the incidence algebra of a (locally finite) weakly ranked poset that satisfy some recurance relations encoded by the poset. 
We are interested in interpreting the polnyomials geometrically.

Let $X$ be a variety over $\mathbb{F}_q$ with a smooth stratification 
\[
        X=\bigsqcup _{p\in P}V_p,
\]
i.e. each stratum $V_p$ is a smooth subvariety of $X$ and the closure of each stratum is a union of strata. We define a partial order on $P$ by putting $p\leq q$ if and only if $V_p\subseteq \overline{V_q}$, and a weak rank function by $r_{pq}=\dim V_p - \dim V_q$.
Proudfoot introduces a framework to construct an element in the incidence algebra of $P$ and gave sufficient conditions for it to be KLS-polynomials. He shows that all Schubert varieties and toric varieties give rise to KLS-polynomials.

In the case of $X$ being a toric variety, Michalek, Monin and Wang consider a different (possibly singular) stratification, the Bialynicki-Birula (BB) decomposition. The same framework of constructing an element in the incidence algebra extends. They show that if the chow quotient of $X$ is rationally smooth, then the constructed element is KLS-polynomials.
They show that the constructed Chow polynomials is the Poincare polynomials of the Chow quotient.

In this paper, we unified the two theory. In the case of $X$ being a $\Gm$-variety, we give sufficient conditions on $X$ such that we can construct KLS-polynomials. We also give sufficient conditions on the Chow quotient of $X$ such that we can construct Chow polynomials.
Finally, we prove that Peterson varieties satisfy the conditions.

\section{Geometric Setup}

Fix a finite field $\mathbb{F}_q$ with algebraic closure $\overline{\mathbb{F}_q}$. Let $l$ be a prime not dividng $q$. For any variety $Z$ over $\mathbb{F}_q$, denote $IC_Z$ the $l$-adic intersection cohomology sheaf on the vareity $Z(\overline{F}_q)$ with the convention that $IC_Z$ is perverse without shifting.

Let $X$ be a projective $\Gm$-variety over $\mathbb{F}_q$ with (finite) isolated fixed points. The positive and negative Bialynicki-Birula cell of $X$ corresponding to a fixed point $p\in X^\Gm$ is defined to be
\[
        X^+_p=\CB{x\in X\mid \lim_{t\to 0}t\cdot x=p}\textnormal{ and }
        X^-_p=\CB{x\in X\mid \lim_{t\to \infty }t\cdot x=p}
\]
respectively. $X^_p$ and $X^-_p$ can be singular in general. 
For each $p,q\in P$, define
\[
        U_{pq}:=X^-_p\cap X^+_q\textnormal{ and }
        Z_{pq}:=X^-_p\cap \overline{X^+_q}.
\]

Further assume that the BB-decomposition 
\[
        X=\bigsqcup _{p\in P}X^+_p
\]
is a stratification, i.e. the closure of each stratum is a union of strata. We define a partial order on $P$ by putting $p\leq q$ if and only if $X^+_p\subseteq \overline{X^+_q}$, and a weak rank function by $r_{pq}=\dim X^+_p - \dim X^+_q$.
Hence 
\[
        \overline{X^+_q}=\bigsqcup_{p\leq q}X_p^+,
\]
Geometrically, a point in $U_{pq}$ gives a $\Gm$-orbit whose $t\rightarrow 0$ endpoint is $q$ and whose $t\rightarrow \infty $ endpoint is $p$, i.e. $p\in \overline{X^+_q}$.
In particular, $U_{pq}\neq \emptyset$ implies $p\leq q$.
Hence
\begin{equation}
        Z_{pr}=\bigsqcup_{p\leq q\leq r}U_{pq}.
\end{equation}

Note that $X^-_p$ is a weighted affine cone with cone point $p$, i.e. the $\mathbb{Z}$-grading on the affine coordinate ring $\mathbb{F}_q[X^-_p]$ induced by the $\Gm$ action is non-negative and the vanishing locus of the ideal of positively graded elements is $p$. And $\overline{X^+_q}$ is a closed $\Gm$-invariant subvariety. Hence $Z_{pr}$ is a weighted affine cone with cone point $p$.

Following the notation of Proudfoot, we write $IH_z^*(Z)$ to denote the cohomology of the stalk of $IC_Z$ at $z\in Z$. For $p,q\in P$, we denote $IH^*_{pq}:=IH^*_{p}(\overline{X^+_q})$. For a weighted affine cone, it is well-known that the stalk cohomology of the IC sheaf at the cone point coincide with the global intersection cohomology (add reference), i.e.
\begin{equation}
        IH_p(Z_{pr})=IH(Z_{pr}).
\end{equation}
